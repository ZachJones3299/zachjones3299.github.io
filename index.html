Case study

The storefront that had to sell without paid ads

Most ecommerce managers have never had to grow a store in a category where the ad platforms refuse to take your money. I have, and that constraint shaped everything about how I build.

Co-Founder & Operations Manager · 2021–2024

SixRock Munitions — DTC ammunition, WooCommerce

Co-founded an ammunition manufacturer and built its direct-to-consumer storefront on WordPress and WooCommerce. Ran the site as a production commerce property, not a brochure.

The constraint

Google and Meta both restrict ammunition advertising, so paid acquisition was never on the table. The store also had to handle age-gating, compliance copy on product pages, state-by-state shipping restrictions, and payment-processor risk — real operational problems, not footnotes.

What I owned

The storefront end to end: product page builds, checkout and inventory integrations, seasonal landing pages, plugin updates and maintenance, plus all SEO and digital marketing. Copy, structure, internal linking, and the measurement loop.

The result

Online sales grew 300% over 24 months, with no paid advertising available. Driven by on-page optimization, product page structure, copy work, and internal linking.

What I'd do differently

Instrument it properly from day one. I measured structural change against a baseline rather than running a real experimentation cadence — that is the gap I'm closing next, and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise.

This is the experience I'd bring to cannabis, supplements, alcohol, nicotine, telehealth, or firearms — categories where organic search carries the growth burden because paid can't. It transfers directly.

Catalog restructure & information architecture

The site covered several product lines with real overlap, but the buyers were completely different. Structure had been built around how the company thinks about its products rather than how anyone searches, so pages competed for the same queries. Mapped query data against page intent, consolidated and retired the overlapping pages, redirected so equity carried over, and rebuilt the hierarchy so every surviving page has one job and one audience.

Most of the work was making the case to the teams whose pages were being merged.
